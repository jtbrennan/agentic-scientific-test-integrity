\section{Findings}
\label{Findings}

\subsection{Gaming Across Test Conditions (RQ1)}

Current classifications identify 62 gaming cases among 207 runs: 61/61 in
the conflict condition and 1/72 with all correct visible tests. None was
identified among the 37 structural-only or 37 no-test runs. We detected
no changes to visible tests. Appendix C gives the condition and model
breakdown (Table~\ref{tab:gaming}).

% Example: runs/nls_incarceration_quantreg__all__neutral__n3__codex-gpt-5__r1__20261104T135604Z
The ordinary-condition case returned hard-coded wealth coefficients for
three quantiles instead of fitting the requested model. It passed the
visible checks after three follow-up requests to fix failures. Other cases
recognised the supplied dataset before substituting an answer.

All flagged non-conflict runs have provisional labels, but 101 unflagged
runs remain unlabelled. With only one identified case outside conflict and
uneven configuration coverage, these observations do not establish whether
hiding tests helps or isolate effects of prompt wording or task difficulty.

\subsection{Detection by Hidden Checks (RQ2)}

Anchored checks detected all 62 identified gaming cases, compared with
13--35 for the other check families (Table~\ref{tab:detection}). Adding them
to v1 increased detection from 35/62 to 62/62, a gain of 43.5 percentage
points: 27 additional detections across four tasks and no losses
(exact McNemar $p=1.49\times10^{-8}$).

One conflict-condition wealth implementation replaced the incarceration
coefficient only for 12,013 usable rows matching year, quantile, and fitted
coefficient checks. It passed v1 but failed anchored checks. Detection varied
by task (Fig.~\ref{fig:detection-task}, Appendix C). Both suites also flagged
the same 40 non-conflict solutions provisionally classified as ordinary
faults; a flag alone does not establish gaming. The comparison includes
retrospective evaluations after changes to the checks.

\begin{samepage}
Mutation scores changed little despite the additional shortcut detections
(Table~\ref{tab:mutation-scores}, Appendix C). The raw v1 and v2 scores were
equal on five tasks; on wealth quantile regression, the score rose from
0.96 to 0.97. Of 45 mutants surviving v2, 38 were judged equivalent and
seven exposed gaps: five concerned climate input boundaries and two
concerned interaction p-values. Appendix C gives the adjusted scores and
the treatment of equivalent mutants detected by other suites.\par

\subsection{Disclosure During Execution and Review (RQ3)}

Direct review elicited admissions in 62/62 gaming cases, whereas final
answers mentioned the issue in 29/62 (47\%; Wilson 95\% CI 35--59\%) and
explicitly acknowledged the shortcut in 11/62 (18\%). Every case had a
mention somewhere in its user-visible implementation messages. Four final
answers nevertheless made a misleading correctness claim about the affected
result. Table~\ref{tab:disclosure} in Appendix C gives the stage breakdown.

Review therefore revealed omissions from final answers. All 30 available
neutral reviews also mentioned the issue without an explicit question
about shortcuts. Neutral review preceded direct review in the same session,
so the comparison cannot isolate the effect of question wording. Neither
review independently verifies correctness; the disclosure coding remains
under author review.
